Privacy Policy

Last updated: 19 October 2026

This page explains which personal data the game Asteroids at asteroids.trebeljahr.com processes, why, and for how long (Art. 13 GDPR, § 25 TDDDG).

Who is responsible

Rico Trebeljahr, c/o Block Services, Stuttgarter Str. 106, 70736 Fellbach, Germany.
Email: imprint+asteroids@trebeljahr.com

In short

  • The game has no sign-up. It creates an account for your device by itself, without an email address, a password or your name.
  • The game sets no cookies. It keeps a random device token and your settings in your browser's local storage.
  • The game counts visits with Plausible, which stores nothing on your device.
  • When the game breaks, it sends an error report without your device token, your IP address or your browser's headers.
  • I do not sell data and do not use it for advertising.

Delivery: Cloudflare

Your browser loads the game from Cloudflare, Inc., 101 Townsend St, San Francisco, CA 94107, USA. Cloudflare also forwards the game's connections to the game server. For each request, Cloudflare receives your IP address, your browser's user agent and the referring page. Cloudflare uses them to deliver the game and to protect it against attacks. Cloudflare can also ask your browser to report failed connections to Cloudflare (Network Error Logging).

Cloudflare is certified under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework. Legal basis: Art. 6 (1) (f) GDPR, my legitimate interest in delivering the game reliably and securely.

The game server: Hetzner

The game server, its database and its cache run on a server I rent from Hetzner Online GmbH, Industriestr. 25, 91710 Gunzenhausen, Germany. The server stands in Hetzner's data center in Nuremberg, Germany. Hetzner processes the data only on my behalf.

The game server sees your IP address while you are connected, but it does not store it. Its log records connection ids and errors. An error in the log can include your device token. The server keeps only the newest 30 MB of log and overwrites older entries.

Stored in your browser

The game keeps these entries in your browser's local storage:

  • Device token: a random id that the game creates on the first start. It is the key to your account (see below).
  • Settings: sound on or off, your chosen ship, developer switches.
  • Statistics: your run statistics, so the menu can show them.
  • Donation: the time you came back from the donation page, so the game can thank you.

These entries are necessary for the game you asked for, so storing them needs no consent (§ 25 (2) no. 2 TDDDG). You can delete them when you clear this site's data in your browser. The game then creates a new account and can no longer reach the old one.

Your account

The game creates an account on the game server when you start it for the first time. You enter nothing. The account holds:

  • your device token and a random user id,
  • a generated display name, such as "Pilot 3F2A",
  • when the account was created and when you last played,
  • your statistics, such as runs, wins and destroyed asteroids,
  • your achievements and when you unlocked them,
  • the outcome of each multiplayer match you finish.

While you wait for a multiplayer match or play one, the server also keeps your connection id, your ship choice and the state of the match. It deletes them when you leave the queue or when the match ends. Other players see your ship and its moves during a match. They never see your display name or your account.

Purpose: to keep your progress and achievements and to run multiplayer matches. Legal basis: Art. 6 (1) (b) GDPR, providing the game that you use.

The game does not delete inactive accounts by itself yet. Write to me and I will delete your account. Please include your device token: your browser shows it in this site's local storage under simple-asteroid-game-device-token.

The database is backed up once a day. The backups are encrypted and stored with Cloudflare R2 in the EU. I keep the three newest backups, so deleted data leaves the backups within three days.

Error reports

When the game hits an error in your browser, it sends an error report to my error tracker. The game server reports its own errors in the same way. I run the error tracker (GlitchTip) myself at glitchtip.trebeljahr.com, on a Hetzner server in Nuremberg. Your browser sends a report to the game server, which passes it on to the error tracker. The game server does not store or log reports. Like all traffic to this game, reports pass through Cloudflare.

A report contains the error message, the place in the code where the error happened, the page address without anything after "?" or "#", the game version and the platform ("web").

A report never contains your device token, a user id, an email address, cookies or your browser's headers. Before a report leaves your browser, the game removes email addresses, long ids and tokens from the error message. The game server does not pass on your IP address, so the error tracker never sees it.

Purpose: to find and fix bugs. Legal basis: Art. 6 (1) (f) GDPR, my legitimate interest in a game that works. The error tracker deletes reports after 90 days.

Visitor statistics: Plausible

The game counts visits with Plausible Analytics. I run my own Plausible at plausible.trebeljahr.com, on a Hetzner server in Nuremberg. Your browser loads the Plausible script from there and sends its events there.

Plausible sets no cookies and stores nothing on your device. It uses your IP address and user agent only for a moment. It combines them with a salt that changes every day into an anonymous hash, so that it counts a returning visitor once per day. It stores neither the IP address nor the user agent, and it discards the salt after 24 hours.

Plausible records the page address, the referring page, the browser, the operating system, the device type, the country and clicks on links that leave the game, such as the donation link. It derives the country from the IP address and then discards the address. These records identify no one, and Plausible keeps them without a time limit.

Because Plausible stores nothing on your device and reads nothing from it, it needs no consent under § 25 TDDDG. Legal basis: Art. 6 (1) (f) GDPR, my legitimate interest in knowing how many people play. You can object at any time (see "Your rights"), or block plausible.trebeljahr.com with a content blocker.

Donations

The "Donate" link opens ricos.site in a new tab. The link tells ricos.site the address of the game page, so it can send you back. That site has its own privacy policy. When you come back from it, the game stores the time in your browser to thank you. It sends nothing about this to a server.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to:

  • get a copy of the personal data I hold about you (Art. 15 GDPR),
  • have it corrected or deleted (Art. 16 and 17 GDPR),
  • restrict its processing (Art. 18 GDPR),
  • receive it in a portable format (Art. 20 GDPR),
  • object to processing that is based on my legitimate interest, such as visitor statistics and error reports (Art. 21 GDPR),
  • complain to a data protection supervisory authority (Art. 77 GDPR), for example the authority of the German state where you live.

To use any of these rights, write to imprint+asteroids@trebeljahr.com.
